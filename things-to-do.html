Things To Do

Museums

National Gallery of Ireland

The National Gallery of Ireland is one of the country's most popular visitor attractions. It houses the nation's collection of European and Irish art from about 1300 to the present day, and serves as an extensive library and archive. The gallery displays work by many artists including:

  • Johannes Vermeer
  • Lavinia Fontana
  • Jack B Yeats
  • Alice Neel
  • Caravaggio
  • Mainie Jellett

Entry to the permanent collection, and many temporary exhibitions, is free for all. The gallery is open seven days a week.

Explore the Grand Gallery via this virtual tour. The Grand Gallery is dedicated to the eighteenth-century Enlightenment and includes works of art by British, Irish, French, Italian, Spanish, Dutch and Swiss artists. Each work of art in the Grand Gallery has a link to Ireland, whether by artist, subject, sitter, former owner or source of acquisition.

National Museum of Ireland

There are four National Museum of Ireland locations.

The National Museum of Ireland, Collins Barracks is home to two fascinating and completely diverse collections. Decorative arts encompasses silver, ceramics, glassware, furniture, clothing, jewellery and coins while the military history collection tells of Ireland's military and revolutionary past. Exhibition highlights include Soldiers and Chiefs, which traces Ireland's military history from 1550 into the 21st century, and Eileen Gray, exploring the life and work of the iconic 20th century designer.

The National Museum of Ireland, Kildare Street is home to an extraordinary range of archaeological artefacts dating from 7000 BC up to the 20th century. The museum collection also includes the Ardagh Chalice, the 'Tara' Brooch and the famous Derrynaflan Hoard. Other highlights include the world-famous Iron Age 'bog bodies', an exhibition of prehistoric Irish gold, and a section dedicated to an early medieval book of psalms known as the Faddan More Psalter.

The National Museum of Ireland, Merrion Street featured galleries of Irish animals and geological exhibits from a collection of around two million specimens. However, the location is closed for refurbishment to ensure that the building is protected, conserved, and made accessible for future generations. Some of the collections are now on display in the Dead Zoo Lab at the National Museum of Ireland, Collins Barracks.

The National Museum of Ireland, Turlough Park, located just outside Castlebar in County Mayo, is the only site outside of Dublin. This museum exhibits the way of life of rural Irish people between 1850 and 1950.

Admission to all four sites of the National Museum of Ireland is free. The museums are open 7 days a week.

Irish Museum of Modern Art (IMMA)

The Irish Museum of Modern Art (IMMA) in Dublin City is a dynamic modern art museum nestled on 48 acres of land in the heart of Dublin 8. The home of IMMA is the historical site of the 17th century Royal Hospital Kilmainham, where it presents ground breaking exhibitions by the most exciting Irish and international artists. IMMA is home to the National Collection of modern and contemporary art, with over 3,500 artworks by Irish and International artists.

Admission to IMMA is free, and entrance to most exhibitions is also free. IMMA is open to the general public 6 days a week; it is closed on Mondays.

Nature & Wildlife

National Botanic Gardens

A premier scientific institution, the National Botanic Gardens contain important collections of plant species and cultivars from all over the world. The Gardens in Dublin are located in Glasnevin, just three kilometres from Dublin City Centre. They are home to over 16,000 plant species and cultivars from a variety of habitats from all around the world.

The gardens are famous for their exquisitely restored historic glasshouses. Most notable are the Turner Curvilinear Range and the Great Palm House, both recipients of the Europa Nostra award for excellence in conservation architecture. Visitors can enjoy such features as the herbaceous borders, rose garden, the alpine yard, the pond area, rock garden and arboretum.

Conservation also plays an important role in the life of the botanic garden. Glasnevin is home to more than 300 endangered plant species from around the world including six species that are already extinct in the wild.

The gardens have been closely associated with their counterpart in Kilmacurragh, County Wicklow, since 1854. Unlike the Wicklow branch, though, they provide a calm and beautiful green space in the midst of the nation's capital.

The Gardens at both Glasnevin and Kilmacurragh are open every day throughout the year, except on the 25th of December (Christmas Day). There is no admission fee to walk and explore.

Dublin Zoo

Dublin Zoo, in the heart of the Phoenix Park, Dublin City, is home to over 400 animals across 70 species. Enjoy keeper talks, seasonal events and family experiences, with every visit supporting vital wildlife conservation worldwide. From majestic elephants to playful primates, Dublin Zoo creates memorable experiences for visitors of all ages.

Dublin Zoo first opened its gates on September 1st, 1831, founded by a small group of scientists and naturalists with a shared curiosity about the natural world. In 1840, featuring 46 mammals and 72 birds donated by London Zoo, the radical decision was to throw its gates open to the public for a penny on Sundays. This gesture, remarkable for that time, established the affectionate relationship that still exists between Dubliners and the 'ah-Zoo' in the Phoenix Park.

Today, Dublin Zoo is a conservation charity that meets the world's highest standards of ethical animal care. As a conservation charity, every ticket purchased directly supports the zoo's mission. 100% of every euro spent at Dublin Zoo helps protect wildlife, preserve habitats, and inspire future generations worldwide.


Historical Sites

Saint Patrick's Cathedral

Saint Patrick's Cathedral is one of Dublin's most visited landmarks. Founded in 1191, its history stretches back to 450 AD. It stands today as one of the few surviving medieval buildings in the city. It is also the burial place of Jonathan Swift, the author of Gulliver's Travels and Dean of the cathedral in the 1700s.

Saint Patrick's Cathedral has been part of Ireland's story for over 800 years. In honour of Ireland's patron saint, it offers a rich cultural and spiritual experience, built on the site where Saint Patrick is said to have baptised early Christian converts 1500 years ago. It is the National Cathedral of the Church of Ireland, and the largest in the country.

Visitors can explore the cathedral through guided or self guided tours available every day with an admission fee.

Dublin Castle

Dublin Castle is located on Castle Street in Dublin City Centre. The history of this city-centre site stretches back to the Viking Age and the castle itself was built in the thirteenth century. The building served as a military fortress, a prison, a treasury and courts of law. For 700 years, from 1204 until independence, it was the seat of English (and then British) rule in Ireland.

Rebuilt as the castle we now know in the seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth and twentieth centuries, Dublin Castle is now a government complex and an arena of state ceremony. The state apartments, undercroft, chapel royal, heritage centre and restaurant are open to visitors.

Dublin Castle will be closed to the public from May 5th to December 31st, 2026 to accommodate the EU Presidency. Otherwise, it is open to the public 7 days a week and all year round.

Libraries

Marsh's Library

Marsh's Library, in Dublin City, is a beautifully preserved building and is one of the few in Ireland still used for its original purpose. Unchanged since it was established in 1707, it is the oldest public library in Ireland. It contains 25,000 rare and fascinating books, as well as 300 manuscripts. Famous readers have included the Irish writers Jonathan Swift, Bram Stoker, and James Joyce.

National Library of Ireland

The National Library of Ireland, founded in 1877, is the library of record for Ireland. The Library houses over 12 million items. It hosts an award winning exhibition about W. B. Yeats as well as children's exhibitions. The Library also hosts a wide variety of events including public lectures, poetry and music recitals, theatre, children's storytelling, creative workshops and much more.

Book of Kells Experience

Trinity College Dublin is Ireland's oldest university and home to its greatest cultural treasure, the Book of Kells. A precious 9th century manuscript containing the four Gospels of the New Testament, the Book is on display as part of the Book of Kells Experience. Located in the Old Library, visitors first view the Book before marvelling at the Long Room, considered to be one of the world's most beautiful libraries. Also on display is Gaia, Luke Jerram's awe inspiring illuminated artwork, which features detailed NASA imagery of the Earth's surface.

Continue your journey into a digital exhibition in the Red Pavilion next to the Old Library, where you will be enchanted as stories, animations and sculptures come to life before your eyes. Interact with spirited sculptures including Ada Lovelace, Jonathan Swift and Rosalind Franklin. Finally, venture on an extraordinary immersive voyage in the Book of Kells 360, a spectacular journey in light and sound of the ancient masterpiece’s history.
